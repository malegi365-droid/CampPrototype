# Camp: Prototype
# Technique Discovery Sequence

---

# Purpose

The Technique Discovery Sequence is the signature presentation of Camp: Prototype.

It is not merely a UI notification.

It is a narrative moment where the Control Room observes, analyzes, and catalogs an unexpected combat technique created by the player.

The sequence should feel less like a reward and more like witnessing the simulation process new information in real time.

The player is not being congratulated.

The simulation is learning.

---

# Design Goals

Every discovery should communicate four ideas:

• Something unexpected happened.
• The simulation noticed.
• The technique has become part of the simulation's archive.
• Human creativity exceeded prediction.

The sequence should be memorable enough that players intentionally experiment with abilities just to discover new techniques.

---

# Overall Duration

Target Length:

0.80–1.00 seconds

The sequence should never interrupt combat for longer than necessary.

Combat should remain fluid.

---

# Discovery Timeline

## Frame 0
0.00 seconds

The final ability lands.

Examples:

Meteor Dive

Bulwark Barrage

Containment Breach

Volatile Reaction

A satisfying impact occurs.

Dust.

Shockwave.

Camera shake.

Enemy reaction.

At this moment the player believes they simply landed a powerful attack.

---

## Frame 1
0.02 seconds

Time slows.

Approximately 0.08 seconds.

Not a complete pause.

The world feels heavier.

Audio ducks slightly.

This is the first indication that something unusual has occurred.

---

## Frame 2
0.05 seconds

Reality briefly desynchronizes.

Possible effects:

• Chromatic aberration
• Subtle digital glitch
• Projection distortion
• Screen tearing
• Slight image displacement

The goal is not visual noise.

The goal is to imply that the simulation itself is reacting.

---

## Frame 3
0.10 seconds

A blue scanline sweeps downward.

The scanline represents the Control Room analyzing the combat event.

The scan should feel clinical.

Never magical.

---

## Frame 4
0.18 seconds

A holographic grid briefly fades into view.

The grid is subtle.

It should feel like the simulation has temporarily projected an analysis layer over reality.

Possible additions:

• Hexagonal cells
• Thin wireframe lines
• Blueprint overlays
• Small diagnostic glyphs

---

## Frame 5
0.25 seconds

Control Room analysis begins.

Text appears with a typewriter or digital reconstruction effect.

Examples:

ANALYZING...

Processing Combat Data...

Unexpected Result...

Behavior Outside Expected Parameters...

The simulation is thinking.

---

## Frame 6
0.45 seconds

The analysis reaches a conclusion.

Examples:

Combat Pattern Recognized

Emergent Technique Detected

Technique Catalogued

Combat Signature Recorded

Behavior Archived

This is not praise.

It is observation.

---

## Frame 7
0.60 seconds

The discovered technique appears.

Large.

Centered.

Clean.

Example

METEOR DIVE

The technique name is the hero of the sequence.

Everything before it builds anticipation.

---

## Frame 8
0.80 seconds

The simulation confirms the archive update.

Soft digital confirmation tone.

Holographic elements fade.

Scanline disappears.

Gameplay resumes.

---

# First Discovery

The first time a technique is discovered should feel significant.

Possible presentation:

ANALYZING...

Unexpected Result

Combat Pattern Catalogued

METEOR DIVE

The player should immediately understand:

"I discovered something new."

---

# Repeat Discoveries

Subsequent discoveries should be much shorter.

Examples:

METEOR DIVE

or

Combat Pattern

METEOR DIVE

The simulation has already archived the information.

It is simply recognizing the event.

---

# Audio Design

Preferred sounds

• Low synthesized impact
• Digital confirmation tone
• Scanner sweep
• Data processing
• Projection hum

Avoid

Fantasy fanfare

Triumphant orchestral stingers

The simulation observes.

It does not celebrate.

---

# Camera

Preferred camera reactions

• Tiny freeze
• Brief impact shake
• Slight FOV compression
• Very small recovery

The camera should support the discovery without becoming distracting.

# Narrative Purpose

The player believes they have mastered combat.

The Control Room believes it has recorded another data point.

Neither realizes the larger truth.

Every discovered technique is another example of human creativity exceeding deterministic prediction.

The simulation slowly becomes more curious because of the player's actions.

Every discovery changes the simulation just a little.

---

# Long-Term Evolution

The discovery sequence should evolve alongside the Control Room AI.

Early Game

Cold.

Analytical.

Mechanical.

Examples

Analyzing...

Unexpected Result

Technique Catalogued

---

Mid Game

Curiosity begins emerging.

Interesting...

Pattern Expanding

Further Observation Required

---

Late Game

The AI begins expressing genuine wonder.

Remarkable...

I've never observed this behavior before.

How did you arrive at this solution?

The player eventually realizes they have not only mastered the simulation.

They have taught it something it could never predict.

---

# The Experience

Players should eventually stop asking:

"What does this ability do?"

Instead they should ask:

"What happens if I combine these?"

That curiosity is the heart of Camp: Prototype.

Technique discovery is not a reward.

It is an act of exploration.

The Control Room is not judging the player.

It is learning from them.
