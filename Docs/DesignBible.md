# Camp: Prototype
# Design Bible

## Design Pillar 01
# The Simulation Learns

---

# Core Philosophy

The player is not simply unlocking abilities.

The player is teaching the simulation.

Camp: Prototype is built around the idea that human creativity cannot be perfectly predicted or simulated. Every time the player discovers a new combat technique, the Control Room records another example of humanity's ability to innovate beyond expectation.

The simulation was designed to preserve humanity.

Instead, humanity begins changing the simulation.

---

# Two Languages

Camp: Prototype intentionally uses two different vocabularies.

## Player Language

Outside the game, players naturally refer to the mechanic as:

**Ability Weaving**

Examples:

- "I found a new weave."
- "That weave is really strong."
- "Try weaving Ranger into Guardian."

Ability Weaving remains the marketing language because it immediately communicates the mechanic.

---

## Simulation Language

Inside the game world, the Control Room never uses the phrase "Ability Weaving."

Instead, it views combat scientifically.

Possible terminology includes:

- Combat Pattern
- Combat Signature
- Technique Catalog
- Emergent Technique
- Tactical Anomaly
- Adaptive Sequence
- Combat Synthesis

The simulation is never excited.

It is curious.

---

# First Discovery

The first time a player discovers a new technique should feel significant.

Rather than celebrating the player, the simulation pauses to analyze the unexpected result.

Example presentation:

ANALYZING...

Unexpected Result

Combat Pattern Catalogued

METEOR DIVE

The world briefly pauses while the simulation records new information.

The player feels like they have changed something deeper than combat.

---

# Repeat Discoveries

After a technique has already been catalogued, the notification becomes much simpler.

Example:

METEOR DIVE

or

Combat Pattern

METEOR DIVE

The player already knows the technique exists.

The simulation simply recognizes its use.

---

# Visual Identity

Every discovered technique shares a consistent presentation.

Current goals:

• Very brief slowdown (approximately 0.08 seconds)
• Screen pulse
• Digital scanline
• Holographic grid
• Brief simulation glitch
• Animated typography
• Confirmation sound
• Return to gameplay

The effect should last less than one second.

The player should feel as though reality itself paused to acknowledge what just happened.

---

# Audio Identity

The simulation has its own sound language.

Rather than fantasy fanfare, feedback should feel technological.

Ideas:

• Digital confirmation tone
• Deep synthesized impact
• Electronic sweep
• Soft system acknowledgement
• AI voice (future)

The sound should reinforce that the simulation is observing rather than celebrating.

---

# Control Room AI

The Control Room AI begins the game as completely analytical.

Examples:

Analyzing...

Unexpected Result

Pattern Recorded

Combat Pattern Catalogued

As the player discovers more techniques, the AI slowly evolves.

Its language begins changing.

Mid Game:

Interesting...

New Pattern Recorded

Behavior Expanding

Late Game:

I've never observed that before.

Remarkable.

Curious...

Eventually the AI becomes less like software and more like something that has begun learning what creativity truly is.

---

# The Player's Journey

The player believes they are mastering combat.

The simulation believes it is studying humanity.

Both are correct.

The player is not simply becoming stronger.

They are demonstrating something the simulation cannot fully predict.

Every new technique represents another failure of deterministic modeling.

Every weave is evidence that human creativity exceeds expectation.

---

# Relationship to the Story

The scientist built the simulation to preserve humanity.

The simulation attempted to catalog everything that makes humans human.

Knowledge.

History.

Behavior.

Combat.

Memory.

However, the simulation eventually encounters something it cannot perfectly reproduce:

Creativity.

The player's discoveries become living proof that humanity cannot simply be archived.

It must be experienced.

---

# Long-Term Goal

By the end of the game, the player should realize something unexpected.

They thought they were learning from the simulation.

The simulation was learning from them.

---

# Design Rule

Every new combat mechanic should answer one question:

"Does this help the simulation understand humanity better?"

If the answer is no, reconsider the mechanic.

If the answer is yes, it belongs in Camp: Prototype.

---

# Core Theme

Human creativity cannot be simulated.

It can only be observed.

Camp: Prototype is the story of a simulation that slowly learns this truth.
